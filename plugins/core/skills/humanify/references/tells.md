# Common tells

Use these as review prompts, not as a banned-word list.
Keep a construction when it adds meaning or matches the established voice.

## Empty framing

Open with the point instead of announcing it.

| Instead of                       | Prefer                 |
|----------------------------------|------------------------|
| "Here's why this matters"        | State why it matters   |
| "It's worth noting that"         | State the useful fact  |
| "In this section, we will cover" | Start with the subject |
| "The key takeaway is"            | Give the takeaway      |

## Formulaic contrast

State the useful idea directly instead of setting up a weak idea only to reject it.

| Instead of                      | Prefer          |
|---------------------------------|-----------------|
| "This is not X. It is Y."       | "This is Y."    |
| "The question is not X, but Y." | Ask or answer Y |
| "Not only X, but also Y."       | "X and Y."      |

## Vague claims

Replace broad praise with the mechanism, result, limit or evidence that supports it.

| Instead of                                 | Prefer                                       |
|--------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| "Provides powerful search."                | Explain what the search matches or filters   |
| "This significantly improves performance." | Name the operation and measured effect       |
| "The system handles errors."               | Describe the error path or recovery behavior |

Words such as *seamless*, *robust*, *intuitive*, *efficient*, *powerful* and *easy* are useful only when the text explains why.

## Generic and padded language

- Remove filler such as "at its core", "in today's world" or "when it comes to" when the sentence works without it
- Prefer a familiar precise word: "use" over "leverage", "explain" over "unpack" and "next" over "moving forward"
- Replace vague actors such as "people" or "the system" when the responsible person, team, component or action is known
- Cut repeated conclusions and lists that add length without adding a new point

## Mechanical rhythm

Repeated sentence openings, equal-length fragments and strings of three-part lists can make prose feel assembled.
Combine or split sentences when that improves the flow.
Do not vary the structure only to make it look varied.

## Formatting as a crutch

Do not use excessive emphasis, exclamation marks, headings, parenthetical notes or dashes to create importance.
Choose punctuation and formatting that make the relationship between ideas clear.
